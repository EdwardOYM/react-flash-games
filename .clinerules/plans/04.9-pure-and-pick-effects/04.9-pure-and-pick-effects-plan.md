> **Checkpoint protocol** (from `05-agent-discipline.md`): run ONE checkpoint per session turn. After each
> checkpoint run the narrowest relevant validation, report *files + behaviour + result*, then STOP and wait
> for "next".
>
> **Scope:** `pokemon-bnb`'s engine only. It continues 04.8, whose choice seam (`PendingChoice`), snapshot
> contract, and effect families are all in place. No new game, no persistence, no protocol change unless a

## Correction to the framing I used in 04.7 and 04.8

I repeatedly described the remaining work as "blocked on player choice or new per-turn state". **Measuring
the actual 58 texts shows that framing was wrong.** About **27 instances across ~18 texts need neither** —
they are pure arithmetic over state the engine already holds, and several are *simpler* than clauses 04.7
already implemented. They were mis-bucketed as "UNCLASSIFIED" or as needing state.

The clearest example: **"This Pokemon also does 30 damage to itself."** appears on three cards (024/054/150)
and was never implemented, while far more intricate 04.7 families were. Self-damage is one clause and one
line.

**Measured backlog after 04.8 (not estimated):**

| Bucket | Instances | Texts | Needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PURE — no picker, no new state** | **~27** | **~18** | nothing; the engine already holds the inputs |
| Visible-zone pick (discard pile, deck top, own Bench) | 16 | 11 | the 04.8 choice seam, over zones already public |
| Heal a chosen Pokémon | 3 | 3 | the choice seam |

**Coverage today: 41 of 99 attack texts parse fully; 58 do not.** All 8 player-triggered Abilities already
work — the 10 unsupported ones are all *passive* ("as long as this Pokemon…", "if this Pokemon has…").

## Progress

- [x] CP0 — Plan written, from a **measurement** of the 58 remaining texts rather than the estimates used
      in 04.7/04.8, which mis-bucketed the pure slice. **Gate: `npm run lint`.**
- [x] **CP1 — the pure slice (~27 inst / ~18 texts).** **Twelve clause kinds**, all reading state the
      engine already holds: `selfDamage`, `spreadOwnBench`, `noWeakness`, `bonusDamageIfDefenderDamaged`,
      `bonusDamageIfHasTool`, `bonusDamagePerHandCard`, `bonusDamagePerBenchWithHp`, `discardTopOfDeck`,
      `opponentShufflesHandAndDraws`, `drawUntilHandSize`, `clearSpecialConditions`,
      `prizesThenShuffleHand`. No picker, no UI, no new state, no snapshot change.
      **104 Ferrothorn went from unsupported to fully parsed** — the headline item, and it exposed a real
      bug rather than just needing a clause. **Two genuine defects the harness caught:**
      (1) **`resolveAttack` returned early on a spread**, so any clause *after* the spread was silently
      dropped — 04.8 CP1 had assumed a spread card carries only the spread. It now sets a flag and skips
      only steps 1–2, so 04.9's Ferrothorn resolves 50-to-each *and* 130-to-itself in one pass. This bug
      was latent in 04.8 and only became reachable once the second clause existed.
      (2) **`bonusDamagePerBenchWithHp` counted the ACTIVE** — the clause says "each of your **BENCHED**
      Pokémon", and `ownInPlay` includes the Active, so the slicing is load-bearing. Caught by a
      control case with no matching Bench.
      **`prizesThenShuffleHand` is ONE clause, not two.** 108/142 read "…take 2 Prize cards. **If you do,
      shuffle your hand into your deck.**" The second sentence fires only on success, and threading that
      across sentences would need cross-sentence state this parser deliberately does not keep — so the
      "If you do" sentence is absorbed, and the harness asserts a **wrong hand size takes no Prizes AND
      does not shuffle the hand**. It also proves the printed order matters: the 2 Prizes enter the hand
      *before* it is shuffled back, so the deck grows by **32**, not 30.
      **`clearSpecialConditions` iterates `STATUS_CONDITIONS`,** not `Object.keys`, so it is exhaustive
      and typed. `noWeakness` is a **flag**, not a bonus: it bypasses `computeAttackDamage` entirely.
      **Recorded fidelity gap, no P2P consequence:** `opponentShufflesHandAndDraws` returns the hand to
      the deck in a deterministic order rather than seeding a shuffle — both peers run identical code over
      identical state so they cannot diverge, but the physical order may differ from a real game.
      **Gate: build + lint + 89-assertion harness (deleted after use) — build clean, lint 0/0, 89/89,
      locale parity 655/655/655, two-way diff 0.** Each family is asserted with an exact number **and a
      no-bonus control** (damaged/undamaged defender, Tool/no Tool, matching/no matching Bench), plus a
      **13-case near-miss suite** — because the engine's standard is that a wrong effect is worse than a
      missing one, and two of my first "near-misses" were number variants of the real text, i.e. not
      near-misses at all, which the suite exposed.
      **Two fixture insights worth keeping:** an **Asleep or Paralyzed attacker cannot declare an attack
      at all** (`cannot-attack`), so the "recovers from all Special Conditions" clause can only be tested
      with the *other* conditions set — both ride the same `STATUS_CONDITIONS` loop. And
      `processAction` returns a **new** state, so several early assertions read the pre-attack object and
      were vacuously true; every zone assertion now reads the returned state.
      **Coverage: 41 → 57 fully-parsing texts, 58 → 42 unsupported** (measured; the plan estimated ~27
      instances across ~18 texts, and the real figure came out higher again).
      **Honest limits:** nothing here has been seen in a browser; the two fidelity gaps above; and the
      engine was still mid-edit when the 89/89 was recorded, so re-run before trusting it.

## Deliberately not split (recorded, so absence is not mistaken for oversight)

- **051 / 124** — "Discard all Lightning Energy from this Pokémon, **and** this attack does 90 damage to 1
  of your opponent's Pokémon." Both halves are *already implemented* and would work — but they are joined
  by a **comma**, so the sentence splitter hands the parser one sentence containing both, and each clause
  pattern is deliberately anchored to the whole sentence. Splitting on a comma is unsafe: it would also cut
  "a Fire Energy, a Water Energy, and a Lightning Energy" (04.6 Lugia) into three broken fragments. **Fixing
  this needs a grammar-aware splitter, not a regex, and is its own future checkpoint.**
- **115** — "Flip a coin. If heads, search your deck for a Pokémon **and switch it with this Pokémon**."
  A search *and* a switch. Search lands in CP4 and switch in CP7; until both exist the compound stays
  unsupported rather than half-applied.
- **069** (Espeon, "Devolve each of your opponent's evolved Pokémon") lands with CP7's board manipulation,
  because it is a de-evolution plus a cross-zone card movement.
- **020** — "Switch this Pokémon with 1 of your Benched Pokémon. If you do, switch out your opponent's
  Active Pokémon to the Bench." Two switches with a condition between them.

## BLOCKED — one product decision, not an engineering one

**Card 116: "Your opponent reveals their hand, and you put an Item card you find there on the bottom of
your opponent's deck."** This is the mirror image of the CP3-A decision and it is the one place where the
game would need to deliberately disclose the **opponent's** hidden zone. 04.8 CP3-A established that a
seat's snapshot never carries the other seat's hand, and this card breaks that on purpose — which is legal
in the real game (the opponent *consents* by having the card), but here the opponent is a remote peer and
cannot consent through a snapshot.

Card **026** ("Your opponent reveals their hand") is the same shape without the card movement.

I am not answering this unilaterally. The options are roughly: keep the hand hidden and leave 026/116
unsupported; reveal the opponent's hand as a **player-consent** toggle in the lobby; or reveal it only to
the acting player for the duration of the choice and never put it in the snapshot (which would need the
host to send an extra out-of-band message). Each has a different cost, and the answer changes the protocol.

## Hard rules

- 01-flash-game-builder: translated copy first; co-located CSS; the 960×540 cap and the landscape/portrait
  breakpoints. CP2–CP4 and CP6 add UI, so they are where those rules bite.
- 02-diagram-flow-checks: **CP5 is the only checkpoint that may change the `Snapshot` shape**, and it must
  update `.github/diagram/architecture-flow.md` in the same change. Single storage key; no `AppConfig`
  change anywhere in 04.9.
- 03-skills: not a new game, so `new-game-project` does not apply.
- 04-terminal-commands: `git --no-pager`, non-interactive, `---done---` sentinels.
- 05-agent-discipline: one checkpoint per turn; throwaway harnesses deleted after each run; **never rewrite
  a harness through PowerShell** (it corrupted UTF-8 in 04.7 CP1 and again in 04.8).
- The engine's standard stands: **a wrong effect is worse than a missing one.** Every pattern stays
  anchored end-to-end, and every new clause needs a near-miss assertion proving it does not fire on a
  neighbouring shape.
