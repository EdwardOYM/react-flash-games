# Implementation Plan — 04.11: the classic cards 159-188

> **Checkpoint protocol** (from `05-agent-discipline.md`): run ONE checkpoint per session turn. After each
> checkpoint run the narrowest relevant validation, report *files + behaviour + result*, then STOP and wait
> for "next".
>
> **Scope:** `pokemon-bnb`'s effect engine only. Not a new game, no persistence, no protocol change unless a
> checkpoint below says so explicitly.
>
> **Why this plan exists:** 04.10 was measured on a **154-attack-text** set and finished at 146. Cards
> 159-188 were then added, taking the set to **188 cards / 195 attack texts / 29 ability texts**. 04.10's
> baseline is therefore stale, and this plan re-measures from the data that is actually there. **04.10 stays
> open** with four original-set texts outstanding; see *Carried from 04.10* below.

**Attack coverage fell from 94.8% (146/154) to 77.9% (152/195), and only 5 of the 30 new cards are fully

## Grouping by what each one actually NEEDS

**The most important finding is the first group: most of the new cards do NOT need new machinery.** 04.6
taught this and CP6 proved it — measuring before reaching for a new effect kind is what stopped a
grammar-aware sentence splitter from fragmenting ten working clauses. **At least 13 of the 43 unsupported
texts are PHRASING VARIANTS of clauses that already exist** (`damageChosenTarget`, `spreadDamage`,
`selfDamage`, `discardEnergy`, `searchAttachEnergy`, `bonusDamagePerEnergyType`, …), usually differing only
by the absence of the words "This attack does". That is a parser-regex job, not an engine job.

### The two decisions only a human can make

**These are recorded as BLOCKERS, not scheduled. Neither is an implementation question.**

1. **Lost Zone** — build it, or rule 176/178 out of scope? The field already exists and is dead, which
   makes "nearly free" *almost* true: it still needs a snapshot wire entry, a UI zone, and the rulebook
   question of whether a card in the Lost Zone is out of the game. That last part is a product rule.
2. **Opponent-hand reveal** — 04.10 refused to decide this unilaterally and nothing has changed except
   the count (2 → 3). The options are still: (a) leave unsupported, (b) a **lobby consent toggle**, or
   (c) an out-of-band reveal to the acting player, which needs a new `NetMessage` and a host-side send
   that is not part of the snapshot. **Group J is not reachable until a human picks one.**

## Progress

- [x] **CP0 — the re-baseline.** Probe run, deleted. 188 cards / 195 attack texts / 29 ability texts;
      43 unsupported attacks (4 original, 39 new) and 7 unsupported abilities (1 original, 6 new),
      grouped above by the mechanism each needs. **Gate: `npm run lint` (0/0).**
- [x] **CP1 — Group A: the PHRASING VARIANTS. 9 texts / 7 cards, ZERO new state.**
      162, 164 (×3), 166, 171 (×2), 172, 185, 187. **The group's premise held: every one
      of these is an effect kind 04.6-04.10 already ships, and the only thing that differed
      was the wording.** One file (`effects.ts`) changed.
      **A PROBE FOUND THE ONE THING GUESSING WOULD HAVE MISSED.** 171 prints "This attack
      does 30 damage to 1 of your opponent's Benched Pokemon" — which looks byte-identical
      to 04.8's clause. It is not: that pattern is
      `^this attack ALSO does (\d+) damage to 1 of your opponent's benched pokemon\.$`,
      because it is the "hits the Active **and also** a Benched" form. Without "also" the
      text matched **nothing at all**, and no amount of reading the card would have shown
      that. **This is CP6's lesson again and it is now the plan's standing rule: probe the
      exact blocking sentence before writing a regex.**
      **Two of the nine needed care rather than a regex.**
      (1) 164's "does 70 damage plus 50 more" — **the card's printed `damage` field is 0**,
      so the 70 exists only in the text. It is pushed as a plain `bonusDamage` (which adds
      to `base`) rather than inventing a `baseDamage` kind, because `base` already starts
      at the printed value and adding *is* the operation. Pushing a nonexistent kind was
      the first attempt and `tsc` caught it.
      (2) 172's "If tails, Pikachu does 10 damage to itself" is the coin-gated form of
      `selfDamage`, which 04.7 defined as UNconditional. `selfDamage` gained an optional
      `coin` flag and the flip happens at the recoil site, so the log can never claim
      damage a heads result never dealt. This is the **only** type change in the group.
      **"Basic Energy" is NOT a type.** 185's filter leaves `energyType` off entirely
      rather than setting it to the word "basic" — which would have matched no Energy at
      all and made the card silently dead while parsing cleanly. The same trap as 042's
      lowercase energy symbols in 04.9.
      **Gate: build + lint + 15-assertion harness (deleted after use) — build clean,
      lint 0/0, tsc clean, 15/15.** Includes **three explicit anti-shadowing regressions**,
      because loosening a pattern to catch a variant is the classic way to break the
      original: 104 Ferrothorn's spread-and-recoil pair, 093's *Defending*-Pokémon lock
      (a different clause shape from 171's "your next turn", and it must not have been
      loosened into it), and 171's own GX attack — which stays **unsupported** on purpose,
      because "per remaining Prize cards" is a genuinely new basis and pretending it
      parsed would be the exact wrong-effect failure this engine ranks worst.
      **Coverage: 152/195 → 161/195. 43 unsupported → 34.** Nine texts off the list in one
      checkpoint, which is the most any single checkpoint in 04.10 achieved.
      **Harness lesson (one, and it is the recurring one).** The probe's first version
      asserted on `ok: []` for every candidate without recording *which* sentence blocked
      it, so the output was uniform and uninformative. Re-deriving the plain sentences
      with the parser's own normalisation — and printing those — is what turned 13
      identical "unsupported" lines into 13 distinct, actionable causes. **A measurement
      that reports a count is a starting point; a measurement that reports the reason is
      the one you can act on.**
- [x] **CP2 — Group B: the damage BASIS. 5 texts / 5 cards, 3 new clause kinds.** 160, 181,
      187, 164, 173. **The probe's hypothesis that Group B was "one genuinely new basis"
      was half right, and the half it got wrong is the useful half.** Three kinds were
      needed, not one, and the split is by *which quantity is being read*:
      (1) `damageTimesEnergy` — a **MULTIPLIER** on the base, in three scopes. Every existing
      damage-maths kind ADDS; "10 damage TIMES the number of Energy" is a different
      operation and folding it into a bonus would have silently turned 10x3 into 13. It
      `assigns` `base` rather than `base +=`, because the additive form would compound with
      a second multiplier. `scope` is the real novelty: 160 counts the attacker's own
      Energy, 181 counts Fairy across **all** your Pokémon, and 187a counts the
      **DEFENDER's** Energy — the only clause in the engine that reads the other side's
      board. Without `scope` as an explicit field, 187a would have been indistinguishable
      from 160 and one of them would have shipped wrong.
      (2) `bonusDamagePerAttachedEnergy` — 164's "10 more damage for each Energy attached
      to <this card>". `bonusDamagePerEnergyType` requires a type and this is UNTYPED;
      `bonusDamagePerTypeCount` counts TYPES. This counts CARDS. 164's printed damage is
      **0**, so the leading 10 is pushed as a plain `bonusDamage`, exactly as CP1's 164b
      did.
      (3) `branchingDamageOnDefenderCounters` — 173's two-sentence form. 173 is the only
      card in the set that gives **two different amounts for the same attack** keyed on a
      board condition, so no existing kind could be reused.
      **173's TWO SENTENCES HAD TO MERGE, and pushing them separately was wrong.** Each
      clause would have had to know about the other, so two independent
      `branchingDamage…` would drift into two half-clauses. The first sentence raises the
      clause and the second patches `damaged` into the *existing* one.
      **Gate: build + lint + 13-assertion harness (deleted after use) — build clean,
      lint 0/0, tsc clean, encoding clean, 13/13.** Assertions cover all three scopes
      separately, because a single multiplier test would pass even with `scope` ignored.
      **Coverage: 161/195 → 166/195. 34 unsupported → 29.**
      **Harness lesson (new, and this one cost four runs).** 173's branch FAILED at first
      with "got 40, want 10" — and the engine was **right**. It dealt 10 to a defender
      already at 30, and damage ACCUMULATES, so the total is 40 either way. The branch was
      real and completely **invisible in the final board**. Two fixtures were also wrong
      before that: `declare()` hard-coded `attackIndex: 0`, which is 173's *other* attack
      ("Group Therapy", which deals no damage), and the fixture sized Energy to attack 0's
      cost, making attack 1 unpayable. **A branch is only observable if the two paths end
      on different totals** — at 5 damage instead of 30 the totals are 40 and 15. This is
      the same lesson as CP1's count-vs-reason, one level down: **assert on the operation,
      not the end state**, because the end state can be identical for a correct
      implementation and a wrong one.
- [x] **CP3 — the COIN-gated damages. 3 texts / 2 cards, 2 new clause kinds, 1 real BUG.**
      165 (×2), 188. **THE PLAN'S GROUP C WAS MIS-FILED, and this is the fifth checkpoint in a
      row where its hypothesis did not survive contact with the text.** CP0 grouped 161/178/182
      together as "player-chosen VARIABLE counts". Re-probing every remaining unsupported text with
      the blocking sentence printed shows otherwise: **182 is not player-chosen at all** — both its
      texts are *derived* ("equal to the number of Water Energy attached", "equal to the number of
      Energy attached to the Defending Pokemon"), which is CP2's basis work, not a new count. 178's
      "as many … as you like" is bundled with moving counters *between* Pokemon, which is the
      plan's own Group E. **Only 161 is a genuine player-chosen count** — and it is the one this
      checkpoint deliberately did NOT do (see below). The probe surfaced 165 and 188, which the
      keyword grouping had filed nowhere useful.
      **165 is a heads MULTIPLIER, which is a different kind from `coinFlipDamage`'s ADD.**
      `coinFlipDamage` would compute 10+10+10 = 30 for three heads where the card prints
      10 x 3 = 30 — the same number at three, and a wrong number everywhere else. It is a new kind
      (`damageTimesHeads`) for the same reason `damageTimesEnergy` was in CP2. Its second form
      ("a coin for each of your Pokemon in play (including this one)") defers the flip count to the
      board, so it reuses 04.10 CP2's `pendingHeadsPerOwnName` slot rather than inventing a state.
      **188 is a gate on a SPECIFIC head count** ("If BOTH of them are heads"), which neither
      `noDamageOnTails` (one flip) nor `bonusDamage`'s `coin` flag (one flip) can express, so
      `bonusDamageIfAllHeads` is strict all-or-nothing — `heads === flips`, never `heads > 0`.
      **THE REAL BUG: A CLAUSE THAT PARSES BUT IS NEVER ROUTED.** `effectTiming` routes damage-
      modifying kinds to `beforeDamage` from a hard-coded allowlist, and my two new kinds were not
      in it, so they classified as `afterDamage` — which step 1 never runs. 165 **parsed perfectly
      and dealt 0 on every seed**, and because a 0 is indistinguishable from tails this would have
      shipped as a working-looking card. CP2's three kinds did not catch this because they were added
      directly into step 1's `else if` chain, which is reached by a different path. **Any new
      amount-changing kind must be added to BOTH places**, and the lesson generalises past 04.11: a
      parser test cannot see this, because the parse is correct.
      **161 IS DELIBERATELY DEFERRED, and it is bigger than the plan's "New state: Yes".** Its
      printed text is "You may discard as many Energy cards as you like … this attack does 30
      damage plus 20 more damage for each Energy card you discarded" — the bonus depends on a count
      the player chooses, but `resolveAttack` applies damage at step 2 and only parks choices at
      step 5. **The engine has no way to defer an attack's damage until a pick completes.** This is
      a new ordering concept, not a new clause, and it wants its own checkpoint.
      **Gate: build + lint + 20-assertion harness (deleted after use) — build clean, lint 0/0, tsc
      clean, encoding clean, 20/20.** **Coverage: 166/195 → 169/195. 29 → 26 unsupported.**
      **Harness lesson (new, and it is about the FIXTURE, not the engine).** Three of the four
      failures in this run were the harness's fault, and all three had the same shape: **the fixture
      paid for the wrong attack.** `battle()` sizes Energy to `costOf(host, idx)`, and omitting
      `attackIndex` defaults `idx` to 0 — so testing 165's *second* attack attached 1 colorless to
      a 2-Darkness attack and every seed returned `insufficient-energy` with 0 damage. The engine
      was right; I had built a board where the attack is illegal. **When an assertion reports a
      suspiciously round 0, check whether the action was even legal before blaming the maths.**
      Two further corrections: (1) the Hydreigon anti-shadowing regression asserted 099 was
      `unsupported`, and failed — 04.10 already supports it via `discardEnergyPerHead`. The
      regression now asserts the specific *kind*, which is what could actually break, because a
      regression built on a stale premise fails for the wrong reason and teaches nothing. (2) The
      4-flip maximum was asserted as "80 is reached" and got 60 — 4 heads in 4 is ~6% per seed, so
      the extreme is flaky while the CAP and the divisibility hold for every seed. **Assert the
      invariant, not the tail.**
- [x] **CP4 — the phrasing variants. 3 texts / 3 cards landed, 1 new condition, ZERO new state.**
      186, 183#1, 184. **CP5 CORRECTED THIS ENTRY: CP4 claimed 4 texts and one of them (183#0)
      never landed** — see CP5 below.
      **The probe found all of them by asking "which existing clause is
      this a near-miss of?" rather than "what does this need?"** — the question that Groups A-D
      kept answering badly was the second one. Two of the three are pure reuses:
      (1) **186 "During your next turn, this Pokemon can't attack"** — `durationCantAttack` has
      shipped since 04.9, and CP1 had already added 171's form the same way. 186 is the THIRD
      inversion of the same sentence and matched none of them, because 04.9's pattern wants "use
      attacks" and 171's wants a trailing "during". **The entire mismatch was the position of one
      preposition**, and no amount of re-reading the card shows that. It is also the clearest
      vindication of the plan's standing rule: probe the exact blocking sentence.
      (2) **184 "isn't affected by ANY effects on your opponent's Active Pokemon"** — 04.9 CP1's
      `noWeakness` required the "Weakness or Resistance, or by" list; 184 drops the list. "Any
      effects" is a strict SUPERSET of Weakness and Resistance, and 184 already rides the flag
      today, so a second kind would be two spellings of one rule that can drift.
      (3) **183's reduction** — 04.9 CP5's `durationLessDamageTaken` with "DONE TO … by attacks"
      added and a trailing "(after applying Weakness and Resistance)". That parenthetical is
      **stripped rather than matched into the clause**: it restates an ordering the engine
      already implements structurally (durations are read after the multiplier), so treating it
      as a condition would be inventing one that can never fail.
      (3) **183's "If the Defending Pokemon is an EVOLVED Pokemon"** — the ONE genuinely new
      thing here, and it is a new *condition*, not a new shape: 164's "is Pokemon-ex" with the
      predicate changed. **It must NOT reuse `bonusDamageIfDefenderIsEx`**, which tests
      `suffix === 'EX'`. A Basic that took an Evolution is evolved and carries no `ex` suffix, so
      the ex clause would fire against the wrong Pokemon AND miss the right one. The test is
      `evolvedTurn > 0` — the engine's existing marker, set at `actions.ts:243`. The printed
      damage is 0, so the leading 50 is a plain `bonusDamage` exactly as 164b's 70 is.
      **`effectTiming` WAS WATCHED THIS TIME.** CP3's bug — a kind that parses but is never
      routed to `beforeDamage`, so it silently resolves nothing — is a trap for EVERY new
      amount-changing kind, and CP4 added one. It is in the allowlist from the start.
      **Gate: build + lint + 21-assertion harness (deleted after use) — build clean, lint 0/0, tsc
      clean, encoding clean, 21/21.** **Coverage: 169/195 → 172/195. 26 → 23 unsupported.**
      **Harness lesson (a VOCABULARY one, and it bit twice in one run).** Both of the last two
      failures were the harness using the wrong NAME for a real thing: the duration's kind is
      `cantAttack`, not `durationCantAttack` (the latter is the *parsed clause*), and the card
      field is `weaknesses` (a list), not `weakness` (a parse-helper's argument name). Neither
      existed, so the assertions filtered to nothing and reported a vacuous pass. **A filter that
      matches nothing is not a passing assertion — assert the count first, then the contents.**
      Two more from this run, both worth keeping:
      - **The `mustDeclare` helper now asserts the action is LEGAL before its damage is read.**
        Every behavioural failure in CP3 AND CP4 traced to a fixture that made the attack
        illegal — once from Energy sized to the wrong attack, once from the index reaching
        `declare` but not `battle`. "Got 0" should always prompt "was the action even legal?"
        first, and the harness now says so out loud instead of leaving it to be inferred.
      - **184's damage figure is NOT assertable, and the harness now says why rather than
        asserting a number that cannot discriminate.** The only Psychic-weak card in the set is
        083 Lucario at 120 HP, and 184 prints 130 — so the correct 130 and the wrong 260 BOTH
        knock it out, and the board afterwards is identical either way. The first version read
        `guest.active` and found a promoted card, concluding nothing. **A fixture that cannot
        fail the test is worse than no fixture**, so the assertion is now the decidable one (the
        flag is present, and alone) with the reason recorded in a comment.
- [x] **CP5 — a CORRECTION, not new coverage. 1 text, 0 new state.** 183#0.
      **THIS CHECKPOINT EXISTS BECAUSE CP4 REPORTED A CARD THAT NEVER LANDED, and the only
      reason anyone noticed is that CP5 re-probed the WHOLE SET before picking its work.**
      CP4's summary said "4 texts / 4 cards"; the coverage delta was **3** (169 → 172), which
      already disagreed with the claim and was not reconciled. CP5's first action was to list
      every remaining unsupported text — and 183#0 was still on it.
      **THE CAUSE IS ONE SPACE.** 183's reduction pattern ended `)?\.?$`, but the `<em>` markup
      leaves a SPACE before the full stop, so the normalised sentence ends "(…Resistance) ." and
      the anchored tail could not match. **04.9's own sibling pattern already writes `\s*\.?$`
      for exactly this reason, and the new pattern was written without copying it.** The fix is
      `\s*\.?$`.
      **THE REAL LESSON IS ABOUT THE CLAIM, NOT THE REGEX.** The CP4 harness asserted
      `parseAttackEffects(183#0)` equalled the expected clause, and that assertion was written
      from the *assumption* that the card parsed rather than from its output — so it did not do
      the job an assertion exists to do. The corroborating signal was sitting in plain sight: the
      coverage delta (3) and the claimed delta (4) did not match, and a delta that disagrees with
      a claim should stop the checkpoint rather than be reported as a pass. **When a checkpoint's
      headline number and its measured number disagree, that is the checkpoint's first finding.**
      **Every checkpoint from here re-derives its own delta from the whole-set count and asserts
      the specific card numbers it claims**, so a silent miss cannot be reported as a pass again.
      **Gate: build + lint + 17-assertion harness (deleted after use) — build clean, lint 0/0, tsc
      clean, encoding clean, 17/17.** **Coverage: 172/195 → 173/195. 23 → 22 unsupported.**
      **174#1 WAS SCOPED AND DELIBERATELY DROPPED.** "Discard all basic Fire Energy or all basic
      Lightning Energy … does 60 damage times the number of Energy cards you discarded" is a
      multiplier on a count produced by the PRECEDING clause — the same ordering wall as 161: the
      discard runs as an after-damage applier while the damage is fixed at step 1. It looks like
      CP2's `damageTimesEnergy` and is not; **it is 161's deferred-damage problem wearing a
      different hat**, and it should land with that mechanism rather than as a parser change.

## Why the ordering is what it is

Ordered by **machinery needed, ascending** — deliberately NOT by instances covered:

1. **Group A** is ~13 texts for zero new state. If the plan stops after one checkpoint, it should be
   this one — and it is also the cheapest proof that the re-baseline is right.
2. **Group B** is arithmetic with one genuinely new basis (the defender's attachments).
3. **C, D, E, F, G** each add exactly one new concept and are independent of each other, so they can be
   taken in any order once A and B are done.
4. **H** should follow **066/152/158** (04.10's last passive): same mechanism, so doing that first makes
   Mew VMAX nearly free.
5. **I and J are last because they are BLOCKED, not because they are hard.** No amount of engineering
   substitutes for the two decisions above.

## Honest limits of this plan

- Grouping is **by keyword over printed text**, so a card can be mis-filed — 176 carries BOTH a Lost Zone
  clause and a hand-reveal clause, and the probe assigned it to the first group that matched. **The
  grouping is a starting point to be corrected against the text, not a count to be trusted.**
- Group A's "~13" is an estimate from a keyword pass, not a per-card audit. It is the number most likely
  to be wrong, and it is also the group that matters most, so CP1 must verify it card by card.
- **Nothing here has been implemented.** Every row is a hypothesis about what a card needs — and this
  plan's own 04.10 record is that such hypotheses have been wrong on contact before: CP5, CP6, CP7 and
  CP8 each found the plan's mechanism prediction incorrect once measured.
